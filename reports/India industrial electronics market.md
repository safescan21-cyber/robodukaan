# Repair-first strategy can win PCBDukaan's market

PCBDukaan should lead with **component-level repair of drives, SMPS, PLC and HMI boards**, sold with transparent prices and a photo quote, and use spare-parts sales as a second income line. The repair side has no large online brand except one Vadodara company (Synchronics). Repair prices are hidden behind "Get Best Price" buttons. And repair saves the customer **roughly 20–60% of the cost of a new unit**. The parts side is crowded and price-driven. On popular branded items such as a Mean Well 120 W DIN power supply, the gap between dealer and online prices is only about 10–15%, so margins there are thin. The markets behind both lines are growing. Research firms put India's VFD (AC drive) market at about **USD 0.5–1.8 billion**, growing about **5–9% a year**. PLCs are about USD 0.5–1.0 billion, HMIs about USD 0.18 billion and automation sensors about USD 0.75 billion. These firms disagree a lot, so treat every figure as a rough range. Demand is strongest in **Tamil Nadu, Gujarat and Maharashtra**, then Karnataka and the NCR/UP/Haryana belt. Most buying is urgent breakdown buying. Buyers send a photo or part number by WhatsApp or phone and want a GST invoice. A small new player can compete if it makes trust visible: real reviews, test videos, clear warranty and published price bands. It should start with prepaid repair jobs and small COD part orders, not big credit accounts.

## Drives grow 5–9% a year, while overall MRO grows below 2%

The first thing to know is that **nobody agrees on the size of India's industrial automation market**. IMARC says **USD 7.57 billion in 2024**, growing 8.6% a year ([IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/india-industrial-automation-market)). Ken Research says **USD 17.28 billion in 2025** ([Ken Research](https://www.kenresearch.com/industry-reports/india-industrial-automation-market.md)). TechSci says USD 16.2 billion in 2024 with 14.8% growth ([TechSci](https://www.techsciresearch.com/report/india-industrial-automation-market/15147.html)). MarketsandMarkets says **USD 24.9 billion in 2025**, growing 8.8% ([MarketsandMarkets](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/Market-Reports/geography/factory-industrial-automation-sme-smb-market/India)). The 3x gap comes from scope: some firms count robots, software, process instruments and services, and some do not. Most forecasts cluster at **8–10% growth a year**. Two (TechSci, Mordor) say 14–15% ([Mordor via GII](https://www.gii.co.jp/report/moi1690929-india-industrial-automation-market-share-analysis.html)). Always say which source and scope you use.

A sensible planning range (**estimate**) is a VFD market of **USD 0.5–1.2 billion (about ₹4,400–10,500 crore at ₹88 per USD)**, growing 6–8% a year. Another useful check: ABB India had record **CY2025 revenue of ₹13,203 crore and orders of ₹14,115 crore, both up 8%**, and said drive products performed well ([ABB India Q4 2025 release](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/corporate/ABB_19022026174454_Press_ReleaseQ42025.pdf)). That one company's size supports the higher VFD estimates rather than the lowest one.

No one publishes a size for the **industrial electronics repair market** in India. Three signals show that the market is real and growing. First, ABB opened its largest drives service workshop in Vadodara in 2023, doubling its service capacity ([ABB](https://new.abb.com/news/detail/105573/abb-india-inaugurates-its-largest-drives-service-workshop-doubling-the-service-capacity)). Second, ABB announced a further services-workshop expansion in March 2026 ([ABB](https://www.abb.com/global/en/news/134015)). Third, Yaskawa India claims 22 authorised service centres ([Yaskawa brochure](https://www.yaskawaindia.in/pdf/drives-motion-control.pdf)). Every drive sold today becomes a repair job in 5–10 years, when its capacitors, fans and IGBTs wear out (**estimate**, based on general industry knowledge). So the 6–9% growth in drives also grows the future repair market. The important contrast is that **automation components grow 5–11% a year, while general MRO grows only about 1.7%**. PCBDukaan's focus on drives, power and control boards is the right part of the market.

Online buying is still a small share. Moglix, the largest industrial e-commerce company, had **₹4,964 crore operating revenue in FY24**, almost flat (+5.5%), with a loss of about ₹189 crore ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/with-rs-4964-cr-revenue-moglix-reports-flat-growth-in-fy24-7348885)). Another source gives a much higher FY24 figure of ₹7,905 crore ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/companies/moglix)), so the sources conflict. Set even the higher number against a USD 56 billion MRO market and online channels have **probably 1–2% or less of MRO spend (estimate)**. But MSMEs are moving online. A 2026 India SME Forum survey says digital channels already handle **about 30–40% of MSME procurement**. It also says **8 in 10 marketplace users finish a purchase within a week, versus 6 in 10 using traditional channels** ([Tribune/PTI](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/world-msme-day-2026-5-in-10-msmes-continue-to-face-supplier-management-and-invoice-compliance-challenges-digital-procurement-adoption-accelerating/)).

## Three states and breakdown buyers drive demand

**Where the factories are.** The latest Annual Survey of Industries (2024-25, released September 2026) counts **41,221 factories in Tamil Nadu, 33,084 in Gujarat and 27,379 in Maharashtra**, the top three (snippet: [DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/news/tamilnadu/at-41221-tamil-nadu-retains-2024-25s-top-spot-for-highest-factory-count)). India's factory count grew about 2.6%, and factory jobs passed 2.1 crore ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/economy/story/asi-2024-25-manufacturing-workforce-crossed-21-million-factories-up-2-6-558882-2026-09-30)). Registered manufacturing output grew **7.81% in FY25** ([DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/registered-manufacturing-sector-grows-7-81-in-2024-25-asi/)). By manufacturing value, Maharashtra (15.9%), Gujarat (14.1%), Tamil Nadu (10.9%), Karnataka and Uttar Pradesh together hold **more than 54%** of India's total ([The Policy Edge](https://www.policyedge.in/p/asi-202425-registered-manufacturing-employs-14-lakh-more-people-as-gva-rises-96)). Maharashtra also has the most Udyam (MSME) registrations, at 13.07% of the national total ([IBEF](https://www.ibef.org/uploads/industry/Infrographics/large/MSME-Infographic-May-2025.pdf)). Industries that run many motors, such as **textiles, cement, steel, plastics and pumps**, use the most VFDs. MarketsandMarkets says energy-saving retrofits in textiles, cement and steel drive VFD demand ([MarketsandMarkets](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/Market-Reports/geography/variable-frequency-drive-market/India)). That makes Surat, Coimbatore, Ludhiana, Rajkot and Tiruppur good targets for drive repair (**estimate**). Ludhiana alone has tens of thousands of MSME units. **Who buys and how.** No survey covers industrial electronics buyers specifically. The segment profiles below are our **estimate**, built on the general MSME evidence:

| Segment | How they buy | What they value | Fit for PCBDukaan |
|---|---|---|---|

The general MSME evidence is sourced. More than half of MSMEs regularly struggle with **many suppliers, non-compliant invoices, credit limits and price swings**. Users choose marketplaces for wider choice (88%) and better prices (76%) ([Tribune/PTI, ISF 2026](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/world-msme-day-2026-5-in-10-msmes-continue-to-face-supplier-management-and-invoice-compliance-challenges-digital-procurement-adoption-accelerating/)). Late payment is a serious problem: ₹22,097 crore was still pending in MSME Samadhaan cases as of December 2025 ([KNN India](https://knnindia.co.in/news/newsdetails/msme/delayed-payments-remain-a-big-challenge-for-msmes-mos-karandlaje)). One rule works in PCBDukaan's favour. Since 1 April 2024, a buyer cannot claim a tax deduction for payments to a registered micro or small supplier that are more than **45 days late** (Section 43B(h)) ([StartupFlora](https://startupflora.com/post/msme-45-day-payment-rule-in-india)). **Registering PCBDukaan on Udyam** therefore helps it get paid on time by bigger customers.

**Channels.** IndiaMART is the main place where industrial buyers search for spares and repair. It has **41 million active buyers and 8.8 million supplier storefronts** (Q1 FY27), but only **about 218,000 paying suppliers** ([Kalkine](https://www.kalkine.co.in/article/announcements/indiamart-nseindiamart-q1-fy27-results-show-10-revenue-growth-and-strong-deferred-revenue-build)). It generates about 27 million unique enquiries a quarter ([AlphaStreet](https://alphastreet.com/india/?p=181725)). Most repair shops in our competitor list sell through IndiaMART. WhatsApp is the most used digital selling tool among MSMEs, at 72%, though that survey is from 2022 ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/smbstory/bluehost-survey-msme-digitisation-covid-19-whatsapp)). Moglix and Industrybuying serve mostly planned purchases by bigger companies. The physical markets serve urgent, cheap component buying. Ritchie Street in Chennai has about 2,000–2,500 shops, much of it grey-market goods from China and Korea ([Citizen Matters](https://citizenmatters.in/ritchie-street-electronic-market-grey-mount-road-brand/)). Lajpat Rai Market in Delhi sells both branded goods and counterfeits (low-quality blog source: [Shiprocket blog](https://www.shiprocket.in/blog/electronic-wholesale-market-in-delhi-best-places-to-buy-in-bulk/)). Lamington Road in Mumbai has PLC and component dealers ([MachineTools.com](https://www.machinetools.com/en/companies/712-brisk-engineering-solutions)). We found **no data on what share each channel has**. Our **estimate** of the order of importance for a new seller: Google search, IndiaMART and WhatsApp first; direct relationships second; MRO marketplaces third; physical markets as both competitor and supplier.

### Repair companies: one online leader and a long tail of IndiaMART shops

**Synchronics Electronics (Vadodara)** is the benchmark. It is the only independent repairer with a large online presence. It claims "since 1994", "1,00,000+ drives repaired" and a "4.8★ / 682 Google reviews" badge. All of this is self-reported, and the badge appears identically on many pages ([Synchronics](https://synchronics.co.in/electronics-repair-vadodara/)). It already offers **pan-India pickup, free evaluation, no-fix-no-fee, 48-hour express and 24-hour emergency service**. It also has city pages (Mumbai, Pune, Delhi, Ahmedabad) and thousands of model-level pages that rank well on Google (snippet: [VFD repair Mumbai](https://synchronics.co.in/vfd-repair-mumbai/); [emergency repair](https://synchronics.co.in/emergency-drive-repair/)). It covers Chinese drive brands too, such as INOVEC, FRECON and VEICHI ([INOVEC page](https://synchronics.co.in/manufacturers/inovec/)). Its weak points are real. All repairs happen centrally in Vadodara, so its own Mumbai page says customers get drives back in **4–5 working days**, not 48 hours. Its warranty is stated as 6 months on some pages and 12 on others. Some pages show placeholder data such as "0+ Repairs Completed" and indexed test items (snippet: [Synchronics ABB listing](https://synchronics.co.in/manufacturers/abb)). This means **copying Synchronics' offer is not enough**. PCBDukaan must beat it on proof, speed and honesty.

The other players are small, mostly single-city shops that sell through IndiaMART. **Rapid Automation (Rapid Automation Sales & Services)** lists AC drive and VFD repair on IndiaMART. **Margins.** We found **no public source for Indian dealer or distributor margins** in industrial automation. Common industry talk of a "10–25% dealer discount" could not be verified. The evidence points two ways (both **estimates**). On popular Chinese-brand items, the gap between IndiaMART dealer prices and online stores is only about **10–15%** (Mean Well EDR at ₹1,958 vs ₹2,199), so resale margins are likely in the **low teens**. On genuine branded imports, Indian prices sit **about 30–80% above the USD list price** after duty, freight and margin, judging from the IGBT example. Hobby stores like Robu already sell generic SSRs and load cells near IndiaMART prices, so PCBDukaan cannot win there on price.

**Selling costs** are documented. IndiaMART's entry Mini Dynamic Catalog costs **₹35,000 a year + GST** ([IndiaMART](https://corporate.indiamart.com/indiamart-paid-service-mdc/)). Higher packages run about **₹1.1–6.5 lakh a year** (brokerage estimate, [Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=455120)). Some plans also offer pay-per-lead at about ₹22–33 per lead (date unclear, [IndiaMART help](https://help.indiamart.com/?p=4674)). Razorpay charges **2% + GST (about 2.36%)** on cards and netbanking ([Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/blog/razorpay-payment-gateway-pricing-explained/)). Courier COD fees are about **₹25–40 or 1.3–2.5% of order value**, whichever is higher ([Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/upstox-originals/investing/is-cash-on-delivery-quietly-costing-you-more/article-184260)). On a ₹5,000 order, COD and prepaid cost about the same (₹65–125 vs ₹118). COD adds the risk of a returned parcel, which roughly doubles the freight (**estimate**). Amazon.in charges 5–10% referral fees in business and industrial categories ([Amazon.in](https://sell.amazon.in/fees-and-pricing)).

## Policy helps local makers but adds compliance work for SMPS sellers

**Make in India and PLI.** The main new scheme is the **Electronics Component Manufacturing Scheme (ECMS), ₹22,919 crore**, approved in March 2025 for six years ([IANS](https://ianslive.in/cabinet-approves-rs-22919-crore-electronics-component-manufacturing-scheme--20250328163303)). Approved projects worth about **USD 4.6 billion** are expected to create about 34,000 jobs ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/economy-and-policy/centre-approves-electronic-component-projects-worth-46-billion)). The scheme mainly targets components for phones, IT and telecom. Its effect on PCBDukaan is indirect: more local parts supply over time, and more electronics factories in Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, UP and Gujarat that will need automation and repair (**estimate**). ABB India is also adding local drive production lines ([ABB](https://new.abb.com/news/detail/130417/abb-india-expands-local-production-capabilities-with-new-line-for-energy-efficient-drives)), which grows the installed base PCBDukaan can repair later.

**BIS.** **Standalone SMPS with output of 48 V or less** must be registered under BIS's Compulsory Registration Scheme (IS 13252 Part 1) since 1 April 2021. Registration is per brand and per model ([BIS Scheme II](https://www.bis.gov.in/product-certification/products-under-compulsory-certification/scheme-ii-registration-scheme); [Agile Regulatory](https://www.agileregulatory.com/blogs/bis-crs-registration-for-standalone-switch-mode-power-supplies-smps-with-output-voltage-48v-max-is-13252-part-1-2010)). This matters a lot. **Selling cheap unregistered Chinese 24 V power supplies is illegal**, and making a private-label SMPS needs testing and registration for each model. Before listing any SMPS, check that it carries a BIS R-number. Whether DIN-rail industrial units are always in scope needs checking on the current BIS list.

**Import duties on Chinese electronics.** Aggregator sites (not checked against official CBIC data) show **20% basic customs duty on HSN 8504 40** (SMPS, inverters, VFDs). With the social welfare surcharge, the non-creditable duty is about 22%, and the total effective rate is about 44–49% including 18% IGST ([Patron Accounting](https://www.patronaccounting.com/hsn-code/85044040)). A GST-registered business can claim back the 18% IGST. Budget 2025-26 cut the number of industrial tariff rates to eight ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/custom-duty/budget-2025-26-customs-proposals-key-changes.html)). Our **estimate** for a USD 20 power supply couriered from China: it lands at about ₹2,465 before GST. That is **more than the Indian street price** of a Mean Well EDR-120-24. So small courier imports of standard power supplies do not pay. Buy branded stock from Indian distributors. **We found no anti-dumping duties** on these products from China, and could not confirm current duty rates for PLCs (8537), relays (8536) or IGBTs (8541). Duty on semiconductor devices under 8541 appears to be nil on one undated table ([BankBazaar](https://www.bankbazaar.com/tax/custom-duty.html)).

**GST and MSME growth.** After the GST rate changes from 22 September 2025, most power supply, inverter and converter goods (HSN 8504) stay at **18% GST** ([Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/hsn-code-8504-product-classification-gst-rate-business-filing-guide/)). The rate for PLCs (8537) was not confirmed. The MSME base is large and growing fast: **over 7.3 crore Udyam registrations** by December 2025 ([The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/business/msme-sector-achieves-key-milestones-in-2025-udyam-registrations-cross-7-3-crore-msme-ministry-1503532923.html)). Many of these are one-person service or trading units, so the factory counts above are a better measure of real demand. Since 2017, new LV motors must meet the IE2 efficiency standard ([ABB](https://new.abb.com/motors-generators/iec-low-voltage-motors/articles/india-bans-low-efficiency-motors-to-save-energy-and-cut-emissions)). Energy-saving pressure keeps pushing factories to add VFDs ([Powerline, 2025](https://powerline.net.in/2025/04/08/gaining-traction-increasing-demand-for-energy-efficient-motors-and-drives/)). We could not confirm whether an IE3 rule is in force in 2026.

## Win the breakdown buyer first, then grow into regular supply

**The opportunity.** PCBDukaan's existing features already match the gaps found in the market. The photo-based repair quote is something no competitor in the notes offers. The fault-code guides for 7 drive brands are another; none of the online stores reviewed had them. GST business checkout and COD answer the top MSME pain point of non-compliant invoices. The recommendations below are **our judgement (estimate)**, built on the evidence above.

**The main risks.** First, **Synchronics already owns Google** for "VFD repair + city" and brand/model searches, so SEO alone will be slow. Second, **trust is hard to earn** in a market full of fake parts and fake claims. Customers will ask why they should send a ₹50,000 drive to a new company. Third, **COD on repair jobs is risky**: a customer can refuse an expensive repaired board at the door, leaving PCBDukaan with the return freight and the parts cost. Fourth, **grey-market and fake parts** can destroy the brand if even one batch slips in. Fifth, **BIS compliance** on SMPS. Sixth, **technician capacity**. Repair quality depends on a few skilled people, and a bad repair costs a customer production time. Seventh, **credit pressure**: OEMs and panel builders expect 30–60 days, and late payment is common across MSMEs.

**Recommended go-to-market plan:**

| Step | What to do | Why (evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Fix basics now | Activate the WhatsApp number. Register on Udyam. Create a Google Business Profile with real photos of the bench and test setup. | WhatsApp is the top MSME digital tool; Udyam brings the 45-day payment protection |
| 2. Publish prices | Put clear **price bands** on each repair page: e.g. SMPS ₹600–1,500, PLC ₹1,000–5,000, HMI ₹2,000–5,000, small VFD ₹2,000–8,000, larger VFD by kW band. Say "final quote after free photo check, no work without your approval". | Competitors hide prices or show misleading "from" prices |
| 3. Out-trust Synchronics | Offer a **6-month warranty, 12 months on drives** once you are confident. Send a short **load-test video** of every repaired unit before dispatch. Show dated Google reviews only. Publish real turnaround and first-time-fix numbers each month. | Independents give 3–6 months, ABB gives 12; Synchronics' claims are inconsistent |
| 4. Set payment rules | **Repair:** free photo check; small refundable advance or prepaid pickup; balance by UPI before return dispatch. **Parts under about ₹5,000:** COD allowed. **Larger part orders:** prepaid or bank transfer. **Credit:** only for repeat GST customers after 3 orders. | COD costs about the same as prepaid but adds return risk; Industrybuying sets the 30-day credit benchmark |
| 5. Pick a focus | Start with **drives (Delta, INVT, Siemens V20, Yaskawa, ABB) and SMPS**. Add **weighing indicators and inverter/UPS cards** as niche pages. | Drives are the biggest and fastest-growing segment; weighing and UPS repair have almost no online competition |
| 6. Target clusters | Spend ads and outreach first in **Tier 1: Pune–Chakan, Mumbai–Thane, Ahmedabad–Rajkot–Surat, Chennai–Hosur–Coimbatore**. Use prepaid reverse pickup from these cities. | Top three states by factories; textile and process clusters use many VFDs |

Before investing heavily, test the plan cheaply. Run one month of Google Ads plus IndiaMART pay-per-lead in two clusters. Record cost per lead, quote-to-order rate, average job value, parts cost per job and technician hours. These numbers will replace the estimates in this report.

## Conclusion

The research changes how PCBDukaan should see itself. It is not mainly an online parts store that also repairs. It is a **trusted repair service that also sells the parts its customers need**. Parts alone put PCBDukaan in a race against Moglix, Robu and thousands of IndiaMART dealers, on thin margins and wide price spreads. Repair puts it against one SEO-heavy company with credibility gaps and a long tail of small shops that hide their prices. The drive market grows 6–9% a year and every installed drive eventually fails, so the repair pool keeps getting bigger. For a small new player the key resource is not capital but **visible proof**: test videos, honest price bands, real reviews and warranties that match the OEM.

Two big unknowns remain, and PCBDukaan should measure them itself: the real **gross margin per repair job**, and the **cost to win one paying customer** through IndiaMART versus Google. Published sources cover neither, and both decide whether the business can grow. The safest path is to keep COD for small part orders only, take prepayment on repair, follow BIS rules strictly on power supplies, and add credit and exchange boards only after the first repeat customers prove they will come back.
